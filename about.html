Bio & research focus

Download CV See Research

I am a sociologist with a PhD from the University of Washington and advanced training in social statistics. My research examines how religious rituals shape national belonging, ethnic boundaries, and intergroup solidarity in multiethnic societies. My dissertation, Sacred Bonds: Ritual, Identity, and Nationhood, develops an interactional theory of belonging through a comparative study of Iran and Turkey. I use mixed methods, combining interviews, archival and newspaper research, comparative-historical analysis, text-as-data methods, and statistical analysis of nationally representative surveys.

My peer-reviewed article, “Making and Transcending Boundaries: The Effect of Ritual on Nationalism,” appeared in Theory and Society in 2024. My current research extends this work through comparative studies of Iran, Turkey, and Belgium, examining how ritual intensity and secularization shape national belonging among dominant and subordinate ethnic groups. I also contribute to public scholarship on identity, protest, ethnic inequality, and environmental justice through research reports, essays, and media interviews.

My teaching spans criminology, drugs and society, social stratification, sociological theory, social movements, and research methods. I have taught at the University of Washington, Cascadia College, Everett Community College, Hofstra University, and the School of the Art Institute of Chicago. Across these institutions, I have designed and taught courses that help students connect sociological theory and empirical evidence to contemporary social problems.
