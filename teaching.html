Teaching

Courses, assistantships, and syllabi

Full CV Contact

I teach sociology as a discipline that tests its ideas against evidence. My courses move between theory and data: students read closely, learn where numbers come from and what they can bear, and practice connecting a claim to the evidence that would support or undermine it. Questions of identity, ritual, protest, and inequality run through most of what I teach, and I ask students to examine those questions in their own communities as well as in the assigned reading.

My courses range from introductory surveys to upper-division seminars — criminology; drugs, law, and society; social stratification; sociological theory; and social movements — taught at research universities, an art school, and two community colleges. That range matters to how I teach: the same material has to reach a first-year student meeting sociology for the first time and a senior preparing for graduate work, and designing for both has made me a more deliberate instructor.
